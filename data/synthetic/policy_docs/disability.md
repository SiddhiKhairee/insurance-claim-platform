# Group Disability Plan Summary

> **SYNTHETIC DATA — for portfolio/demo purposes only.** This document was generated for the
> Group Claims Pipeline portfolio project. It is not a real Mutual of Omaha or any other
> insurer's policy document, and describes no real coverage.

## Coverage Limits

The Group Disability Plan reimburses eligible short-term disability claims up to a maximum of
$5,000.00 per claim. Claims requesting more than this amount are denied automatically by the
plan's benefit cap, regardless of the underlying medical circumstances, the treating provider's
recommendation, or how compelling the supporting documentation is. The requested amount is
generally calculated as a percentage of the member's regular wages for the period the member was
unable to work, capped at the plan's weekly benefit schedule; the plan does not reimburse lost
bonuses, commissions, or overtime pay that would have been speculative rather than guaranteed.
There is no separate annual aggregate cap beyond the per-claim limit in this synthetic plan —
each claim is evaluated independently against the $5,000.00 ceiling, and a member who files
several claims within the same plan year, for example one claim for a short illness in the
spring and an unrelated claim for a minor injury later in the year, is not subject to a running
total across those separate claims. Partial reimbursement up to the cap is not offered; a claim
requesting more than $5,000.00 is denied in full rather than paid up to the maximum, since the
plan is not designed to auto-adjust a requested amount downward to fit within the benefit cap.
A claim spanning a partial work week is prorated based on the number of scheduled work days
missed, rounded to the nearest whole dollar, and this proration happens before the cap is
applied, not after. Members are encouraged, though not required, to itemize the basis for the
requested amount when submitting a claim, since a clear itemization can help the claims team
distinguish a legitimate high-cost claim from a data-entry error, even though it does not change
how the cap itself is applied to the final requested figure.

## Exclusions

The plan does not cover disabilities resulting from self-inflicted injury, participation in an
illegal act, or a pre-existing condition diagnosed and treated within the 90 days immediately
before the enrollment's effective date. Disability arising from normal, uncomplicated pregnancy
is covered under a separate maternity rider and is excluded from this plan's base benefit,
meaning a claim filed for pregnancy-related disability under the base plan will be denied even
though a nearly identical claim filed correctly under the maternity rider might be approved.
Disability arising primarily from a diagnosed substance use disorder is excluded unless the
member was enrolled in an approved treatment program at the time the disability began, in which
case the claim is evaluated under the plan's standard rules rather than being excluded outright.
Cosmetic procedures and any disability arising directly from a cosmetic procedure are excluded
entirely, including complications from an elective cosmetic surgery performed outside the
member's home region, and including recovery time from a procedure the member describes as
medically motivated if a licensed provider classifies it as cosmetic. Disability sustained while
engaging in a hazardous recreational activity not disclosed at enrollment, such as skydiving,
scuba diving beyond recreational certification limits, or professional-level motor racing, is
excluded, though ordinary recreational activities like hiking, cycling, or amateur team sports
are not considered hazardous for this purpose. Claims submitted more than 180 days after the
date of disability onset are denied for late filing, independent of medical eligibility — the
plan treats the 180-day filing window as a hard administrative boundary rather than a guideline
subject to case-by-case exceptions, even when the delay was caused by the disability itself.

## Waiting Periods

A newly enrolled employee must wait 30 days from the enrollment's effective date before any
disability claim becomes eligible for benefits. This waiting period exists to discourage
enrollment timed to a known, imminent disability event, and applies uniformly regardless of the
employee's prior disability history with a previous employer's plan or any continuous-coverage
credit that employee might have accrued elsewhere. The waiting period is waived entirely for
disabilities resulting from a workplace accident reported to the employer within 48 hours of
occurrence, since these are, by construction, not foreseeable at the time of enrollment and
therefore do not present the same anti-selection risk the waiting period is meant to address; a
workplace accident reported later than 48 hours, absent a documented reason such as the member
being unconscious or hospitalized immediately after the accident, does not qualify for the
waiver and reverts to the standard 30-day rule. An employee who re-enrolls after a lapse in
coverage is treated as newly enrolled for waiting-period purposes, and does not retain credit
for time served under a prior, lapsed enrollment period, even if the lapse was brief or caused
by an administrative delay rather than the employee's own choice. The waiting period is measured
strictly from the enrollment's effective date, not the date the employee's paperwork was
processed or the date the employee was notified their enrollment was approved. A disability
whose onset date is genuinely ambiguous, such as a gradually worsening repetitive-strain
condition rather than a single identifiable injury, is evaluated using the date a licensed
provider first formally diagnosed the condition as disabling, not an earlier date on which the
employee merely noticed symptoms.

## Definitions

"Active coverage" means an enrollment record with status `ACTIVE` as of the date the claim's
underlying disability began, not the date the claim was submitted — a claim can therefore be
denied for lack of coverage even if the member's enrollment was active at the moment of filing,
if it had lapsed or not yet begun on the actual date of disability onset. "Short-term
disability" means an inability to perform the material duties of the employee's own occupation,
meaning the specific job the employee held immediately before the disability began, for a period
expected to last between 14 and 180 days; this plan does not apply the stricter "any occupation"
standard some disability products use after an initial period, under which a claim could be
denied if the member could perform some other, different job. Disabilities expected to last
longer than 180 days fall outside this plan's scope and are not addressed by this synthetic
document, since a real long-term disability product would typically be structured as a separate
plan with its own eligibility rules, elimination period, and benefit schedule. "Material duties"
means the core functions of the role as described in the employee's job description at the time
the disability began, not incidental tasks the employee may have occasionally performed outside
that description. "Elimination period" is a term used in some disability products for the
window between disability onset and benefit eligibility; this synthetic plan uses the simpler
term "waiting period" throughout and does not distinguish the two concepts separately.
