# Group Disability Plan Summary

> **SYNTHETIC DATA — for portfolio/demo purposes only.** This document was generated for the
> Group Claims Pipeline portfolio project. It is not a real Mutual of Omaha or any other
> insurer's policy document, and describes no real coverage.

## Coverage Limits

The Group Disability Plan reimburses eligible short-term disability claims up to a maximum of
$5,000.00 per claim. Claims requesting more than this amount are denied automatically by the
plan's benefit cap, regardless of the underlying medical circumstances, the treating provider's
recommendation, or how compelling the supporting documentation is. There is no separate annual
aggregate cap beyond the per-claim limit in this synthetic plan — each claim is evaluated
independently against the $5,000.00 ceiling, and a member who files several claims within the
same plan year is not subject to a running total across those claims. Partial reimbursement up
to the cap is not offered; a claim exceeding the limit is denied in full rather than paid up to
the maximum, since the plan is not designed to auto-adjust a requested amount downward to fit
within the benefit cap. Members are encouraged, though not required, to itemize the basis for
the requested amount when submitting a claim, since a clear itemization can help the claims
team distinguish a legitimate high-cost claim from a data-entry error, even though it does not
change how the cap itself is applied.

## Exclusions

The plan does not cover disabilities resulting from self-inflicted injury, participation in an
illegal act, or a pre-existing condition diagnosed and treated within the 90 days immediately
before the enrollment's effective date. Disability arising from normal, uncomplicated pregnancy
is covered under a separate maternity rider and is excluded from this plan's base benefit,
meaning a claim filed for pregnancy-related disability under the base plan will be denied even
though a nearly identical claim filed correctly under the maternity rider might be approved.
Cosmetic procedures and any disability arising directly from a cosmetic procedure are excluded
entirely, including complications from an elective cosmetic surgery performed outside the
member's home region. Claims submitted more than 180 days after the date of disability onset
are denied for late filing, independent of medical eligibility — the plan treats the 180-day
filing window as a hard administrative boundary rather than a guideline subject to case-by-case
exceptions.

## Waiting Periods

A newly enrolled employee must wait 30 days from the enrollment's effective date before any
disability claim becomes eligible for benefits. This waiting period exists to discourage
enrollment timed to a known, imminent disability event, and applies uniformly regardless of the
employee's prior disability history with a previous employer's plan. The waiting period is
waived entirely for disabilities resulting from a workplace accident reported to the employer
within 48 hours of occurrence, since these are, by construction, not foreseeable at the time of
enrollment and therefore do not present the same anti-selection risk the waiting period is
meant to address. An employee who re-enrolls after a lapse in coverage is treated as newly
enrolled for waiting-period purposes, and does not retain credit for time served under a prior,
lapsed enrollment period.

## Definitions

"Active coverage" means an enrollment record with status `ACTIVE` as of the date the claim's
underlying disability began, not the date the claim was submitted — a claim can therefore be
denied for lack of coverage even if the member's enrollment was active at the moment of filing,
if it had lapsed or not yet begun on the actual date of disability onset. "Short-term
disability" means an inability to perform the material duties of the employee's own occupation
for a period expected to last between 14 and 180 days; disabilities expected to last longer than
180 days fall outside this plan's scope and are not addressed by this synthetic document, since
a real long-term disability product would typically be structured as a separate plan with its
own eligibility rules and benefit schedule.
