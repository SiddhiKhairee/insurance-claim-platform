# Group Vision Plan Summary

> **SYNTHETIC DATA — for portfolio/demo purposes only.** This document was generated for the
> Group Claims Pipeline portfolio project. It is not a real Mutual of Omaha or any other
> insurer's policy document, and describes no real coverage.

## Coverage Limits

The Group Vision Plan reimburses eligible vision claims up to a maximum of $500.00 per claim.
This limit covers eye exams, prescription eyeglasses (frames and lenses), lens upgrades such as
anti-glare coating or progressive lenses, and contact lenses in combination, not as separate
sub-limits, in this synthetic plan — a member who needs both an exam and new eyeglasses in the
same visit files one claim covering both, still capped at $500.00 total rather than $500.00 per
item. A claim requesting more than $500.00 is denied in full against the plan's cap rather than
partially reimbursed up to the limit, and the plan does not average the exam cost separately
from the eyewear cost, or the frame cost separately from the lens cost, to bring any individual
component under the cap on its own. There is no separate annual aggregate cap beyond the
per-claim limit, so a member who needs a second, unrelated vision claim later in the same plan
year, for example a lost-and-replaced pair of glasses or a new prescription following a change
in eyesight, files it as an independent claim evaluated on its own merits against the full
$500.00 limit again. A contact lens fitting fee charged separately from the lenses themselves is
treated as part of the same claim as the lenses it relates to, not as a standalone eligible
expense, and is included in the total measured against the cap.

## Exclusions

Laser vision correction surgery (LASIK and similar refractive procedures) is excluded from this
plan, as it is treated as an elective procedure rather than corrective eyewear, even when a
member's optometrist recommends it as a long-term alternative to glasses or contacts. Safety
glasses or protective eyewear required for a member's job duties are excluded from this personal
vision benefit, since such equipment is expected to be provided or reimbursed separately by the
employer as workplace safety equipment rather than personal health coverage. Non-prescription
(cosmetic-only) contact lenses and sunglasses without a corrective prescription are excluded,
since the plan's purpose is to offset the cost of vision correction rather than eyewear in
general, though prescription sunglasses used as a member's only pair of corrective eyewear are
covered under the standard eyeglasses benefit. Replacement eyewear purchased solely for style
rather than due to a change in prescription or physical damage is not covered, and a member
cannot file a second claim for replacement frames within the same plan year absent a qualifying
prescription change or a documented damage event such as a broken frame. Claims submitted more
than 180 days after the date of service are denied for late filing, measured from the date of
purchase or exam, not the date the invoice was paid or the date a payment plan for the eyewear
was completed. Eyewear purchased through an online retailer using a prescription issued by a
provider outside the plan's typical network is not excluded on that basis alone, provided the
prescribing provider is licensed and the prescription is current under the 24-month rule
described under Definitions below.

## Waiting Periods

A newly enrolled employee must wait 30 days from the enrollment's effective date before any
vision claim becomes eligible for benefits. Unlike the dental plan's tiered waiting periods,
this synthetic vision plan applies a single flat 30-day waiting period to all covered services,
since vision claims in this plan design do not distinguish between routine and complex care the
way dental claims do, and there is no equivalent of a "major service" category carrying an
extended waiting period here — an eye exam and a set of progressive lenses are both subject to
exactly the same 30-day window. A member who re-enrolls after a gap in coverage is treated as
newly enrolled for this purpose and does not carry over any waiting-period credit from a prior
enrollment period, consistent with how the other lines of coverage in this synthetic plan family
handle re-enrollment. The waiting period is measured from the enrollment's effective date
regardless of when the member's most recent eye exam under a prior plan took place, so a member
switching employers mid-year does not receive credit for time elapsed since their last exam
under a previous employer's vision benefit. If an employee's eye exam is scheduled before the
30-day mark but the eyewear itself is not ordered and billed until after the waiting period has
closed, the claim for the eyewear is still evaluated against the effective date on file rather
than being denied for the exam having occurred too early, since the exam and the eyewear can be
billed as separate line items on the same overall claim. An employee whose plan year renews
mid-enrollment does not face a second waiting period at renewal; the 30-day window applies once,
at initial enrollment, and does not reset annually the way some benefit maximums do.

## Definitions

"Active coverage" means an enrollment record with status `ACTIVE` as of the date of service, not
the date the claim was submitted, so coverage that lapses after the date of service but before
the claim is filed does not retroactively disqualify an otherwise eligible claim. "Eligible
vision claim" means an eye exam performed by a licensed optometrist or ophthalmologist, or
prescription eyewear (glasses or contact lenses) issued pursuant to a current prescription no
more than 24 months old at the time of purchase, reflecting the plan's assumption that
prescriptions older than that are unlikely to still accurately reflect the member's vision.
"Contact lens fitting fee" means the charge for the exam and follow-up visits needed to
determine the correct lens curvature and fit, distinct from the base eye exam fee, but treated
as part of the same eligible claim when billed alongside contact lenses rather than as a
standalone service. "Lens upgrade" means an optional feature added to standard corrective
lenses, such as anti-glare coating, photochromic tinting, or a progressive (no-line bifocal)
design, and is treated as part of the same eligible claim as the base lenses rather than a
separate line item subject to its own cap. "Frames" means the physical structure holding the
lenses, evaluated as part of the same combined eyewear claim as the lenses themselves; a member
who purchases frames only, intending to reuse lenses from a prior pair, may still file a claim
for the frames alone under this same benefit.
